Kvass

Rye, time, and a little sourness.

What is kvass?

Kvass is a lightly fermented drink from Eastern Europe, made from rye bread or rye malt, water and sugar, with yeast and lactic acid bacteria doing the work. The fermentation is kept short, so it stays low in alcohol: sweet and sour, gently sparkling, and tasting of dark bread.

People have been drinking it for more than a thousand years. The Primary Chronicle mentions kvass at the celebrations of 988, it was sold from barrels in the street, and it is still the base of okroshka, a cold summer soup.

How ours is different

Fermented with raw makgeolli

Instead of baker’s yeast, our kvass is fermented with raw Korean makgeolli. Along with its yeast, makgeolli carries lactic acid bacteria, which add a soft lactic tartness and keep the sourness balanced.

Cane sugar, not honey or molasses

We ferment with cane sugar rather than honey or molasses, so the flavor stays clear and crisp, and very easy to drink.
